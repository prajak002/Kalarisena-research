E Recovery-to-Standing 0% genuine recovery

From a randomized, physically-settled fallen pose - no reference motion, no tracking target - reach and hold an upright stance within 300 steps. The originally reported "15% success" turned out to be measuring the wrong thing: the success check only tested torso orientation (upright_cos > 0.75), never height - a robot can satisfy it while still curled on the ground. Verified directly on this exact checkpoint: at the moment of a "successful" episode, real base height was 0.19m against 0.78m standing. Fixed the criterion to require real height too (src/envs/recovery_env.py); re-evaluated across 30 episodes on the unchanged checkpoint: 0% genuine success, mean peak height reached 0.285m, about a third of standing. This clip shows exactly that pattern - torso orientation climbs toward upright while height stays near the floor the whole time. A fresh 20M-step run against the corrected criterion has since finished: 0% success, mean max-upright 0.61, every one of 40 eval episodes running the full 300-step timeout without ever sustaining real height and orientation together. Proper training time plus the corrected criterion still doesn't produce genuine standing recovery.

The central message

This is a rigorous, honestly-measured diagnostic study of transferring Kalaripayattu movement to a Unitree G1 through a six-stage RL curriculum, in a MuJoCo/Pinocchio physics stack - not a demo of a solved problem. Under a correctly-measured evaluation protocol, nearly every stage still shows the robot falling - including doing nothing at all (a plain PD controller also falls 100% of the time on these motions). That is a genuine, unsolved result about how hard dynamic martial-arts movement is for this platform, not a failure to report cleanly.

One thing survives close scrutiny as a real, if partial, positive: Stage D measurably softens the impact when a fall does happen (real ~38% mean reduction, corrected from a wrong number). Stage E does not yet - its original "15% success" was measuring torso orientation alone, satisfiable while still on the ground; corrected to actually require standing height, the same checkpoint's real rate is 0%. That correction happened live, today, before this page shipped with the wrong number in it - which is itself the point being made throughout this project: a claimed result gets believed only after someone checks what it actually measured.

Just as important as any single stage's number: today's own measurement process found and fixed three separate, systemic evaluation bugs - episodes starting from random points instead of frame 0 (inflating a reported 83% fall rate to a false positive), color-channel and cutoff bugs hiding the real content of every annotated video, and an early-termination bug that understated Stage D's real impact forces by roughly 100x. Each one, uncorrected, made the project look better than it is. That measurement discipline - not any one clip above - is the paper's real contribution today.

  • Reduce fall rate: not yet achieved by any of six independent approaches tried (PD baseline, Stage A alone, expanded corpus, Stage B isolated, Stage A + balance reward, static-stance curriculum).
  • Make falls recoverable: not yet - Stage D softens impact, but Stage E's genuine standing-recovery rate is 0%, confirmed after a full 20M-step run against the corrected height-aware criterion. Still the most promising open lever, since it is the only stage whose reward already points in the right direction - it just hasn't crossed the bar yet.
